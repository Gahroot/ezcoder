# Shared language

**Motion recipe** — A focused skill defining a particular animation approach, its invariant mechanisms and its permitted changes. A selected recipe may require exact source choreography; it is not merely optional inspiration.

**Source-backed recipe** — A motion recipe grounded in an inspected project and matching reference render. Decoded values, inferred behaviour and visually verified results must be distinguished; source-backed does not mean fully render-verified.

**Support skill** — Instructions for execution, brand identity, source intake or validation. It supports the selected recipe rather than adding another creative style or approval workflow.

**Executable template** — A working animation implementation with defined inputs. A skill containing instructions or extracted animation data is not an executable template until those mechanisms are implemented.

**Input binding** — Mapping the user's text, fonts, colours, logos and media into slots the recipe permits. It does not authorize changing locked timing, effects or choreography.

**Recipe authoring** — The separate developer workflow that inspects a source project, extracts evidence, identifies gaps and creates a reusable recipe. Its private methods and working artifacts are not normal video-runtime dependencies.

**Composition-level choreography** — Coordinated changes in attention, hierarchy, spatial relationships and visual state over time. Judge a reconstruction against its chosen recipe; fixed framing and intentional holds are not universal defects.

**Motion language** — The relationships between rhythm, typography, framing, transformation and continuity that give a video its character. It is independent of subject, palette or a universally mandatory effect.

**Motion study** — A short example demonstrating transferable motion decisions. A study is not binding choreography unless explicitly chosen as a reproduction reference or turned into a source-backed recipe.

**Production depth** — The existing quick, standard and production review scopes. They preserve bounded review and legacy long-project support, not a mandatory multi-document planning process or a change to model/effort.

**Action plan** — A description of opening state, focal action, resulting state and handoff. Useful when the recipe or explicitly authorized custom work needs it, not a competing direction for an already-defined animation.

**Studio preferences** — Optional, validated user preferences and reference choices. They fill unresolved choices without overriding a recipe's locked choreography or importing general coding-agent instructions.

**Review-ready** — Current source/render identity, passing technical checks and image critique without unresolved findings. Not human approval, full playback, audio listening or a guarantee of fidelity/taste. Missing evidence is unverified; budget exhaustion permits an honest draft, not silent success.
